About this data

  • Voting records start in 2023 (the 118th Congress). Congress.gov doesn't publish earlier House votes in a form this site can use.
  • Votes are updated every night. Each representative's sponsored bills and contact details are refreshed once a week, so a brand-new bill can take up to a week to appear.
  • The Vote column shows one vote for each bill. If the House voted on the bill once, the button below it reads “See the vote.” If the House voted on it more than once (for example, on amendments or procedural motions as well as the bill itself), the button reads “See all” and the number of votes, and opens the full list, newest first.
  • When a bill had several votes, the Vote column shows the most recent vote to pass or approve it. If there was no such vote, it shows the most recent vote of any kind.
  • Procedural votes on “rules” (resolutions that set the terms of debate for other bills) are hidden unless you choose to show them.
  • “Not Voting” means the representative didn't cast a vote. “Present” means they were there but chose not to vote yes or no. Some votes are recorded as “Aye” and “No” instead of “Yea” and “Nay”; they mean the same thing.
  • Sponsored bills include the representative's whole career when you choose “All years.”
  • Districts come from the U.S. Census Bureau's current maps. After districts are redrawn, an address can briefly show a new district before its representative takes office.
